Abstract

Social media fake news verification requires models to reason over both textual content and complex social propagation structures. Although large language models (LLMs) have strong semantic reasoning capability, directly feeding complete social cascades into models inevitably brings noisy context, excessive token overhead and limited window constraints.

To tackle these practical limitations, we propose GLANCE, a Global-to-Local Agentic Navigation framework. Multiple lightweight agents explore the whole propagation graph from diverse perspectives, and a budget-aware aggregation module constructs compact local evidence graph for reliable LLM-based authenticity judgment.

Quantitative experiments demonstrate that our method maintains competitive Macro-F1 performance, meanwhile cutting input token consumption by more than 73% compared with naive full-cascade prompt strategy.
